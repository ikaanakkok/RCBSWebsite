A company's share price is really the present value of the money it will earn in the future. When interest rates rise, those future earnings are discounted back at a higher rate, and the stock's "fair" value falls.

## Expectations are everything

Markets react not to the decision itself but to how far it differs from what was expected. A hike that everyone saw coming is usually already priced in.

> Buy the rumour, sell the news.

## Which sectors are most sensitive?

- **Technology:** Most of its earnings lie in the future, so it is the most rate-sensitive.
- **Banks:** Higher rates can widen lending margins.
- **Real estate:** As borrowing costs rise, demand slows.
